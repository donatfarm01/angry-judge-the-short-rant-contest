Build notes for the Angry Judge short rant entry.

The bounty asks for one short funny rant about a small everyday annoyance,
judged pairwise by a furious judge who weighs, in order: whether it is a
real rant about an everyday annoyance, whether it is actually funny, how
specific and concrete the annoyance is, how sharp and economical the
wording is, and how fresh the angle is. The gate allows 1 to 400
characters of UTF-8 text.

I picked the self-checkout machine as the target. It is concrete and
universally shared: the machine scans the milk, weighs it, then still
accuses the shopper of theft in front of a queue. I avoided the examples
the brief itself names, printer, slow bus and Monday morning, because the
field will be crowded with those.

The rant is written in short declarative sentences so the punch comes fast.
It opens on the accusation, escalates through the absurdity of paying in
front of a machine that watched the payment, and lands on the closing
line, which reframes a grocery run as a trial. The entry is original
text written for this bounty.

The program holds the rant as a single constant, validates it against the
400 character gate, writes it to the output path, and prints its length
and hashes so the result can be checked. The entry was run locally and
the output verified against the gate before committing.
